Flow Mapping Assignment

This flow map was designed to emphasize both the magnitude and direction of family migration/relationship flows in the United States from 1887 –1924. The goal behind the mapping decisions was to minimize visual clutter but also to show clear data representation. Different symbology was intentionally selected for nodes, flow lines, and the choropleth background to establish visual contrast and improve readability. The map includes a north arrow in the upper right corner of the map, along with a legend underneath it. The scalebar is in the bottom left corner, along with a reference to the projected coordinate system of the map. The title of the map sits at the top: “FamilyTree Flows 1887-1924". Blue proportional symbols were used for the nodes given that blue is a neutral color and is easy to distinguish from the warmer flow hues. The flow lines were symbolized using a yellow-orange–red color scheme to emphasize movement intensity and directional relationships between locations. Warm colors naturally attract visual attention and are commonly associated with increasing magnitude or activity. Lighter yellow tones represent smaller or weaker flows, while darker orange and red tones represent stronger or more significant flows. The sequential color progression helps readers quickly identify dominant migration or connection patterns across the map. The contrast between the cool blue nodes and flow lines. A light grey choropleth base map was included to provide geographic context and support interpretation of spatial patterns.

The dominant direction of flows appears to go North to South. This pattern seen primarily between the southern and northern halves of the central United States. There is some movement east to west, but that is very centralized to specific regions, and between only a couple states at a time. The interpretation of the flow patterns reveals several important geographic trends. Certain locations act as major destinations that receive the highest number of incoming flows, while other locations function as major origins that send large volumes of flows outward. These directional trends could be reflecting historical migration routes, transportation corridors, population centers, or social and economic connections between places

The flow patterns also correspond to characteristics represented by the node symbols and choropleth background. Areas with larger proportional symbols appear to coincide with stronger incoming and outgoing flows, indicating central or highly connected locations within the network. Similarly, choropleth patterns help explain how geographic or demographic characteristics relate to the observed movement structure. Overall, the combination of proportional symbols and flow lines in this choropleth map creates a balanced and interpretable visualization of spatial flow relationships
